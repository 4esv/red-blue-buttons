Where the world lands

Two views of the same simulation, painted on the world. At dictator-game altruism (a = 0.28), each coloured country gets the prediction that pairs with its World Values Survey trust score. The left map is intentions — who tries to press blue. The right map is outcomes — who survives. Together they make the trap zone visible.

% who'd press blue at a = 0.28

% surviving at a = 0.28

The geographic clustering is the point: trust is a regional habit. Scandinavia, the Netherlands, and parts of East Asia tint lavender on the left — high-trust populations have the most blue voters. The same countries turn brick on the right because they're in the trap zone: enough commit to blue to die for it, not enough to make it win. Latin America and most of the Anglosphere stay sage on the right because almost nobody intends blue.
