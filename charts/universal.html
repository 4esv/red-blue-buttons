Universal blue saves everyone. Universal red saves almost everyone.

Two thought-experiment worlds, each with 1000 people and a 5% misclick rate. In the universal-blue world, the 95% who press blue carry the vote and everyone lives. In the universal-red world, blue loses, and the 5% who fumbled the button die. The asymmetry is the moral case for blue — and the next section is why universalization isn't actually on the menu.

Numbers come straight from the rule: blue wins iff at least 50% of buttons end up blue, and only red voters survive when blue loses. Universal-blue intent × 5% noise = 95% blue, blue wins, body count 0. Universal-red intent × 5% noise = 5% blue, blue loses, body count 50 (the misclickers). The empirics section refutes that universalization is realistic, but here, on its own terms, blue dominates.
